Home-energy offers can describe very different things: educational material, project plans, equipment, installation guidance or combinations of these. Before comparing a headline savings number, identify exactly what the offer contains.

This guide explains how to research an Energy Revolution System offer without treating seller estimates as guaranteed results. It does not independently verify the seller's performance or savings claims.

## 1. Identify what you are actually buying

Start with the current seller page.

Determine whether the offer provides:

- digital instructions or plans
- educational material
- equipment
- a complete system
- support or consultation
- additional products or upgrades

The purchase process is much easier to evaluate when the deliverables are concrete.

## 2. Separate savings estimates from your own energy use

A general claim about saving money is not a guaranteed result for every property.

Actual economics can depend on electricity rates, household usage, climate, equipment efficiency, installation quality, maintenance, incentives and local requirements.

Before accepting a savings estimate, compare the assumptions with your recent utility bills and the specific conditions of your property.

The U.S. Department of Energy's consumer resources discuss home energy efficiency and energy audits as tools for understanding where energy is used and where improvements may be possible.

## 3. Calculate total project cost

A low headline purchase price may not represent the full cost of implementing an energy project.

Check for:

**Upfront purchase:** the price of the digital product or equipment.

**Materials:** additional components or supplies.

**Installation:** professional labor where required.

**Maintenance:** servicing and replacement parts.

**Operating costs:** electricity, monitoring or other ongoing costs.

**Permits or local requirements:** costs that may apply depending on the project.

Compare the expected benefit with the total implementation cost, not merely the price of the information or product that gets you started.

## 4. Check compatibility

A plan or product may not be appropriate for every property.

Questions worth asking include:

- Is the equipment compatible with your existing system?
- Does the project require changes to electrical infrastructure?
- Are there local building or utility requirements?
- Is professional installation required?
- What site conditions affect performance?

When electrical or structural work is involved, use appropriately qualified professionals and verify current local requirements.

## 5. Read the assumptions behind the promise

If a seller provides a projected saving, ask what assumptions support it.

For example, an estimate might depend on usage levels, local electricity rates, expected production, equipment performance or climate.

The more assumptions there are, the more important it is to compare the estimate with your own situation.

## 6. Compare alternatives before buying

A buyer should not compare one seller only against "doing nothing."

Depending on the project, alternatives may include improving insulation or air sealing, updating inefficient equipment, changing usage patterns, or obtaining a professional energy assessment.

The relevant question is: what option provides a reasonable fit for my property, budget and objective?

## 7. Check seller policies

Before checkout, review:

- refund policy
- cancellation terms
- customer support
- delivery or access method
- digital license terms
- equipment warranty information, if applicable
- recurring charges
- optional upgrades

Save the current terms for any purchase that has a significant cost.

## 8. Watch for guaranteed savings language

Energy performance varies by property and project conditions.

Treat phrases such as "guaranteed savings" or extremely precise return claims as prompts for additional questions. Ask for the assumptions, measurement method and conditions under which the estimate applies.

A forecast is not the same as a measured result.

## What this guide does not claim

This article does not claim that the Energy Revolution System will reduce every household's energy bill by a particular percentage. It also does not confirm that a specific project is suitable for every home.

The purpose is to help buyers evaluate an offer against their own numbers and requirements.

## Bottom line

Identify the actual product, compare the seller's assumptions with your own energy use, calculate the full project cost and verify compatibility and local requirements before purchasing. Treat savings estimates as estimates rather than promises.
