Choosing sunscreen gets easier when the label is treated as a checklist rather than a marketing headline.

The American Academy of Dermatology recommends looking for three essentials: broad-spectrum protection, SPF 30 or higher and water resistance.

## Start with the three essentials

When comparing products, check for:

**Broad spectrum:** protection against UVA and UVB radiation.

**SPF 30 or higher:** the minimum discussed in AAD consumer guidance.

**Water resistant:** useful for situations involving swimming or sweating.

Then consider whether the product is practical for your normal routine.

## Match the format to your routine

Sunscreen can come in lotions, creams, gels, sticks and sprays.

The useful format is often the one you can apply thoroughly and consistently.

Check the product's label instructions instead of assuming that one format is automatically better.

## Consider skin type and tolerance

AAD guidance notes that sunscreen formulated for your skin type can make it easier to use.

People with oily or acne-prone skin may look for non-comedogenic labeling when available. Fragrance and ingredient preferences may matter for sensitive skin.

## Think about real-life use

Ask:

- Face, body or both?
- Daily use, exercise or swimming?
- Easy to reapply?
- Works with makeup if needed?
- Practical for travel?

A sunscreen that stays unused in a drawer is not a good practical fit.

## Application matters

The AAD recommends applying sunscreen before going outdoors and reapplying as needed, including after swimming or sweating.

Follow the specific product label directions.

## Compare value, not just price

Compare:

**Cost per ounce or milliliter**

**Amount needed**

**Reapplication needs**

**Shipping**

**Container design**

**Return policy**

A larger package is not automatically better value if the formula does not suit you.

## Separate cosmetic claims from protection criteria

Words such as "glowing" or "anti-aging" are marketing features. Start with the protection information first, then compare texture, finish and other cosmetic preferences.

## When to seek professional advice

Persistent skin problems or significant reactions may require professional evaluation. General buying information is not individualized medical care.

## Beginner checklist

Confirm:

**Broad spectrum**

**SPF 30 or higher**

**Water resistant when appropriate**

**Fits your skin type**

**Practical application**

**Reasonable total cost**

**Clear directions**

## Bottom line

Start with the protection basics, then compare skin type, format, use case and cost. The most useful sunscreen is one you can apply correctly and use consistently.
