Small businesses rarely need every AI product available. The useful purchase is usually the one that removes a defined bottleneck with acceptable cost and review effort.

This guide provides a framework for comparing tools across marketing, support, research, operations and productivity.

## Start with the bottleneck

List the tasks that repeatedly consume time.

Common examples include:

- answering routine customer questions,
- drafting marketing material,
- summarizing meetings,
- organizing research,
- preparing reports,
- scheduling follow-up,
- transforming documents into structured notes.

Choose one task first. A tool should have a clear job inside an existing workflow.

## Measure the full cost

Subscription price is only one component.

A realistic calculation includes:

subscription + setup time + training + human review + integration work

A cheap product can become expensive if every output needs substantial correction.

Likewise, a more expensive product can be worthwhile when it removes many hours of predictable work.

## Evaluate the workflow, not just the feature list

Ask:

- How easy is initial setup?
- Does it integrate with the software already used?
- Can information be exported?
- Are usage limits clear?
- What happens when the limit is reached?
- Can the service be cancelled easily?
- What support is available?

The answers matter more than a long list of AI capabilities.

## Support and customer communication

AI can help draft routine responses or classify requests, but customer-facing automation should have clear escalation paths.

A good system should make it easy for a human to step in when a request is unusual, sensitive or high-impact.

## Marketing

Marketing tools should be evaluated against actual distribution and conversion problems.

For example, a tool that generates twenty headline variations is less useful than one that reliably fits into the site's editorial process.

See [SEO versus paid traffic](/articles/05-seo-vs-paid-traffic/) when deciding which acquisition problem you are actually trying to solve.

## Data considerations

Business information can include customer data, financial records, credentials and confidential plans.

Before using an AI service with sensitive material, review the provider's privacy, security, retention and data-handling information.

Use the least amount of sensitive information necessary for the task.

## Avoid buying for novelty

AI products change quickly. A feature that sounds impressive may not solve a recurring business problem.

The strongest reason to buy is usually measurable workflow improvement.

## Bottom line

Choose the AI product that solves a defined problem with predictable cost, acceptable review effort and appropriate data controls. Verify current pricing, terms and feature availability directly with the seller before making a purchase.
